# Re-run of 2 September 2026 — what changed since 13–14 August

Two independent things changed at once, and they are worth keeping apart:

1. **A new GenBank screening.** Same query, same parameters, three weeks later.
2. **A new pipeline version.** One methodological change to the barcode module.

Everything below is reproducible: `run_config.json`, `pipeline_log.txt` and a
per-record `manifest.csv` are written for both runs, and
`figures/compare_runs.py` / `figures/compare_barcode.py` regenerate this diff.

---

## 1 · Methods change: a separation check in the barcode module

**What was wrong.** Confidence tiers in the barcode mapping measured only how
*close* a fragment sat to its nearest reference, never whether that reference
was distinguishable from the runner-up. Because ST10 was split into ST42–44 on
a ~2% full-length threshold, and a ~600 bp window cannot resolve that, a
fragment could sit inside the "high" band of two different subtypes at once.
In the August run, **100% of ST44 calls and 84% of ST43 calls** had another
member of the ST10/42–44 family in their own top-5 hits; a quarter were exact
ties. The top-5 consensus rule never fired, because a 3-to-2 split still agrees
with the nearest hit.

**What changed.** `map_short_reads.py` gains `--margin` (default 0.01). Every
call must now beat the nearest *differently-labelled* reference by more than
that margin. Failing calls are either collapsed to an agreed complex label
(`COMPLEXES`, currently `ST10-complex = {ST10, ST42, ST43, ST44}`) or, where no
complex is defined, downgraded to `ambiguous`. The manifest carries a new
`separation_note` column recording which sibling caused each collapse and by
what gap.

*(Raised by Eleni Gentekaki. The full-length numbers behind it: ST43 sits 1.83%
from ST10 and ST44 2.18%, while ST10's own internal spread is 1.88% — larger
than its distance to ST43.)*

**Effect.**

| | Aug (no check) | Sep (with check) |
|---|---|---|
| Confident barcode calls | 15,571 (92.8%) | 15,181 (90.3%) |
| `ambiguous` | 40 | 450 |
| ST44 | 174 | 57 |
| ST43 | 58 | 48 |
| ST42 | 403 | 397 |
| ST10 | 799 | 760 |
| **ST10-complex** | — | **178** |
| Novel-cluster support | 121 records / 11 clusters | **68 records / 6 clusters** |

ST42 barely moves, which fits its 3.2–3.9% separation from the other three: it
is the one member of the group a barcode can actually resolve.

The drop in novel-cluster support is the more consequential half. The clusters
that lost corroboration were all colliding with an **accepted subtype**
(novel_5 vs ST4, novel_8 vs ST3, novel_4 vs ST12, novel_9 vs ST7, novel_11 vs
ST1), so those records were never evidence of novelty. **The chelonian lineage
is untouched: 12 records, 7 at high confidence, before and after.**

## 3 · Caution: cluster labels renumber between runs

`novel_N` labels are assigned in size order per run and are **not stable
identifiers**. Between these two runs, the disappearance of the old `novel_1`
shifted 48 records' labels by one, with no biological change whatsoever:

- **chelonian lineage: `novel_3` → `novel_2`**
- ***Castor fiber* cluster: `novel_10` → `novel_9`**

All figure scripts now locate these two clusters **by host composition**, never
by label, and `compare_runs.py` reports them the same way. Any text referring
to a cluster by number needs re-checking against the run it describes.
